Games Like Super Meat Boy: Platformers With Fast Restarts

Super Meat Boy gives you very little time to dwell on a missed jump. You die, reappear, and try a slightly different approach. N++, VVVVVV and Electronic Super Joy all share that quick return to the action, though their movement feels quite different.

These six games keep the gap between a mistake and another attempt short. Some restart the level; others put you back at a checkpoint. That distinction matters: a quick respawn can still mean repeating a long stretch of platforming.

1. N++

N++ makes momentum the centre of the problem. Getting across a room depends on the speed you bring into a jump, the angle you take past a hazard, and where you land. Gold pulls you away from the simplest route to the exit, so finishing a level and finding a satisfying route through it can become separate goals.

Deaths lead straight back into practice. Its episode structure is the wrinkle: several levels form a sequence, so a level clear is not always the same as finishing a standalone stage. Compared with Super Meat Boy, there is more emphasis on preserving speed and judging arcs through open space.

Details: N++ on Steam.

2. VVVVVV

VVVVVV removes jumping. You reverse gravity while standing on a surface, then steer as you fall towards the opposite side of the room. A corridor of spikes becomes a question of when to flip and how far to move sideways.

Frequent checkpoints keep most mistakes local. The connection to Super Meat Boy is the rhythm of seeing a hazard, failing, and immediately trying the same passage again. The movement itself takes a different kind of adjustment: you are controlling a fall rather than shaping a jump. There is also a connected world to explore between the harder rooms.

Details: VVVVVV on Steam.

3. Electronic Super Joy

Electronic Super Joy surrounds its platforming with bright silhouettes, electronic music and deliberately crude jokes. Missiles, low-gravity sections and changes in orientation keep the obstacles from settling into one pattern. Jumping and a downward smash are part of the movement vocabulary.

Checkpoints break levels into repeatable sections, so a death usually returns you to the current stretch rather than the beginning of the whole course. Its presentation is much busier than N++ or VVVVVV. The music is a large part of the atmosphere, but this is a platformer rather than a requirement to press every button on the beat.

Details: Electronic Super Joy on the developer's itch.io page.

4. Celeste

Celeste builds routes around jumping, climbing and an air dash. Many rooms ask you to work out where to use the dash and where you can recover it. Respawning nearby lets you practise that sequence without much interruption.

Compared with Super Meat Boy, there is more room for pauses between movement decisions, and more story between stretches of platforming. Optional strawberries and later challenges make familiar rooms harder without making every extra task necessary to finish the main route.

Details: Celeste on Steam. There are more comparisons in our games like Celeste article.

5. The End Is Nigh

The End Is Nigh, by Edmund McMillen and Tyler Glaiel, follows a small character through connected screens full of hazards. Ledge grabs and carefully placed jumps give it a different feel from Meat Boy's wall sliding.

Ordinary deaths return you to the current screen, keeping the obstacle you are learning close at hand. Moving through a connected world gives the game a different structure from selecting individual courses. Optional collectibles can also ask for a more awkward route than simply reaching the next screen.

Details: The End Is Nigh on Steam.

6. iZBOT

iZBOT uses double jumps and wall slides across short, hazard-filled levels. Death brings a quick respawn, and attempts tend to centre on learning a compact sequence of jumps.

It shares Super Meat Boy's short-course structure, though the extra jump changes how you correct your position in the air. Our game page describes the mechanics in more detail.

Details: iZBOT on Steam.

Fast Respawns and Short Runbacks

Respawn speed is only half of what makes repeated attempts manageable. Checkpoint placement determines how much you replay. VVVVVV often saves progress close to a challenge; Super Meat Boy and iZBOT build around restarting short courses. N++ adds the wider context of an episode.

Those differences are more useful than a single difficulty ranking. A game can be hard in a way you enjoy practising, or ask you to repeat a section you have already solved. The comparisons here describe the normal progression modes; optional challenges may change the rules.
